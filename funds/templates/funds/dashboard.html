Coverage notes
How to read this
Where funds are registered, not where they invest. ECB statistics count a fund in its country of registration. The chart shows the size of each country's real estate fund industry, not how much of its property is held by funds. Luxembourg (4–5× GDP) is left out for this reason; Ireland and the Netherlands are affected to a lesser degree.

Funds only, not listed property companies. REITs and listed property companies are not investment funds and are not counted. Countries where property is mostly held that way (e.g. Belgium, Spain, Sweden) look smaller than their market is.

Mixed sources. Countries marked * have asset figures from a source other than the ECB. Definitions may differ, so compare them with care.

Breaks in the series. Some jumps (e.g. Finland 2014, Germany 2015, Spain 2014) probably reflect changes in reporting or coverage rather than market moves.

Funds + listed real estate view. Adds the market value of REITs and listed property companies (EPRA Total Markets Table, year end, converted from USD at the ECB rate) to fund total assets. Available 2019–2025 (earlier EPRA year-end tables are not publicly available). The two parts measure different things (fund figures include borrowed money, market value does not), funds may own shares of listed companies (some double counting), and a country with no fund figure counts its funds as 0. Read it as an indication of overall size, not an exact total.

Total assets. Figures include property bought with borrowed money, so funds that borrow more look bigger. Assets are year-end stocks; the latest year uses the latest quarter and the previous year's GDP.

% of GDP over time

Fund assets: ECB Data Portal, Investment fund statistics (IVF), real estate funds, total assets, end of period, unless another source is noted for a country; GDP: Eurostat, current prices. Year values use December stocks; the latest year uses the latest quarter and the previous year's GDP. Euro area average = sum of fund assets ÷ sum of GDP of the countries that used the euro in that year, excluding Luxembourg. Listed real estate: EPRA Total Markets Table (year end), converted from USD at ECB rates. Analysis: Elena Kulik, Colliers Lithuania.
